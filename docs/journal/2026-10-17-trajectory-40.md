## 1. The programme as written

**[Fact]** Future Paths ([index](https://cog.spin.systems/future-paths), subtitled "What I wish
Python paths had") is five essays written over three days in January 2026:

| part | title | date | central proposal |
|---|---|---|---|
| [1](https://cog.spin.systems/future-paths-types) | Semantic Types | 18 Jan | Paths that carry intent: file vs directory, must/must-not exist, `Under[ROOT]`, capabilities (`Readable`, `Writable`, `MayCreate`), late-bound `PathVar`s, fragments placed `under()` a parent, lenses, lineage, content addressing, `PathQ` set queries, directory schemas, and schema *inference* over filesystems after polars-genson |
| [2](https://cog.spin.systems/future-paths-connectivity) | Connectivity | 18 Jan | Relations as values: `base`/`base_rel`, `rebase`, `sibling`, `cousin`, dependents moving with their source, five requirements (relation type, base tracking, rebasing, composition rules, type integration) |
| [3](https://cog.spin.systems/future-paths-template-strings) | Deferred Path-ification | 18 Jan | Python 3.14 t-strings with `Param` sentinels: a path tree whose leaves keep their format specs (`{idx:04d}`) until bindings arrive. A proof-of-concept [gist](https://gist.github.com/lmmx/f5d1b07d266f160f9a431c1f6bdc8a17) |
| [4](https://cog.spin.systems/future-paths-compilation-theory) | Compilation Theory | 20 Jan | Optimisation as term rewriting (termination, confluence), register machines, SSA, common-subexpression elimination. E-graphs and Datalog held in reserve. "Python is our parser" |
| [5](https://cog.spin.systems/future-paths-lazy-execution) | Lazy Execution | 20 Jan | A Rust arena IR, a fixed-point optimiser, a slot-machine executor and `compile_path()` via PyO3. "Build once, resolve many" |

A [design report](https://gist.github.com/lmmx/bb2ad52a9efee015c3cd2a083ef3d63d), agent-written at the author's request and dated 19 January, sits between Parts 3 and 5. It mapped wend's nodes onto Polars' DSL/IR/physical-plan split and proposed a crate named `pathforge`.

**[Interpretation]** The series has one thesis, which the retrospective compressed as *a path should be a plan, not a value*. pathlib composition produces a concrete value immediately, and three things are lost in the process:

- intent: what kind of path this is;
- relation: what it is relative to;
- parameterisation: which parts vary.

Parts 1 and 2 are about what a plan should *mean*: types and relations. Parts 3 to 5 are about how to *build and execute* one: deferral, rewriting and compilation. The essays are frank that the first half is speculative ("So far, so much pie in the sky", Part 1). The second half is concrete enough to include Rust signatures.

One idea the essays never state is worth noting. **[Fact]** None of the five parts proposes the reverse direction: parsing a concrete path back into its parameter values. The nearest are Part 1's lens `backward`, Part 1's inference, and Part 3's remark that t-strings "have all the moving parts we would need to infer filename patterns". The retrospective proposed `match`/`glob` as an open thread ([retrospective-17, *Open threads*](2026-10-17-retrospective-17.md#open-threads)), and the rebuild made it central.

## 2. What shipped in February, and what that taught

**[Fact]** wend 0.1.0 and 0.1.1 were published on 3 February 2026. Each was a single
290-line module, the Part 3 proof of concept plus Part 4's three rewrite rules:
- parent–join cancellation;
- literal folding;
- suffix-chain collapse.

None of Part 5's Rust reached the repository or either release ([retrospective-17, *Provenance*](2026-10-17-retrospective-17.md#provenance)).

The retrospective, written eight months later, found three things.

- **[Fact]** The rewrites were unsound. They checked a node's *type* where the rule needed a
  property of its *value*: parent–join cancellation is valid only if the right operand is one
  segment. So `(root / "a/b").parent` gave `/x` where pathlib gives `/x/a`. Seven probe
  expressions disagreed with wend's own un-rewritten nodes ([retrospective-17, *Soundness*](2026-10-17-retrospective-17.md#soundness-of-the-construction-time-rewrites)).
  Parts 4 and 5 had asserted termination and confluence but never stated a soundness condition.
- **[Fact]** Between Part 3 and Part 5 the design had lost t-string semantics. The design
  report's executor had a single slot type, `PathBuf`, so `{idx:04d}` had nothing to apply to.
  Part 5's API (`T("chunk_{idx}.parquet", ["idx"])` with `"idx": f"{i:04d}"`) moved formatting
  back to the caller and re-parsed a template that Python had already parsed ([retrospective-17, *The t-string regression*](2026-10-17-retrospective-17.md#the-t-string-regression-between-the-design-report-and-part-5)).
- **[Fact]** Performance estimates were optimistic by an order of magnitude. The report
  estimated about 1 µs per resolution; 0.1.1 measured about 9 µs. PyO3 ≥ 0.25 converts a
  returned `PathBuf` by constructing a `pathlib.Path`, which bounds what a per-item Rust resolve
  can save ([retrospective-17, *Performance*](2026-10-17-retrospective-17.md#performance)).

**[Interpretation]** All three failures share a cause. The 0.1 tree had no notion of *how many segments a node produces*, and no notion of the *value* domain (ints, strings, formatted text) as distinct from the *path* domain. The rewrites assumed one segment per node, and the Rust design assumed paths all the way down. The rebuild's diagnosis starts from that observation ([rebuild-22, *The architectural diagnosis*](2026-10-17-rebuild-22.md#the-architectural-diagnosis)).

## 3. The rebuild

**[Fact]** The rebuild was merged as five squash commits on 17 October 2026:

| PR | commit | content |
|---|---|---|
| [#18](https://github.com/lmmx/wend/pull/18) | `4d547a4` | the segment normal form, parameter kinds, `bind`, matching, compilation, the semantic contract |
| [#19](https://github.com/lmmx/wend/pull/19) | `a6c2d01` | `glob`, `Lens` |
| [#20](https://github.com/lmmx/wend/pull/20) | `aabe16f` | `wend.frame` (Polars), `bench/bench.py` |
| [#21](https://github.com/lmmx/wend/pull/21) | `5db87d3` | `wend.infer` |
| [#22](https://github.com/lmmx/wend/pull/22) | `3a53182` | README, migration guide, examples, journal |

The decisions that shaped it, in the order they were forced:

- **Segment structure as the invariant.** An expression is a head plus parts. Each `Seg` part
  renders to exactly one canonical segment, and each `Splice` part is a declared sub-path. A
  t-string's literal text is split at `/` when the expression is built, so the segment count of
  `t"data/{split}/chunk_{idx:04d}.parquet"` is known from source, with Python as the parser, as
  Part 4 concluded ([`src/wend/_expr.py`](../../src/wend/_expr.py)). Every operation is then
  *static* (the structure decides it), *deferred* (pathlib decides it after binding) or
  *rejected* (invalid for every binding). This replaced rewriting: no rule picks a static answer
  that values could contradict.
- **Hybrid semantics.** For every binding the expression accepts, results equal eager pathlib.
  Bindings that would let pathlib silently change the structure are refused. This settled the
  retrospective's open choice between (A) a pathlib-compatible API and (B) an independently
  specified algebra by taking (A) on every accepted input and (B)'s ability to refuse.
  Randomised testing against an eager-pathlib oracle forced three refinements:
  - structural elimination: `(root / seg).parent` needs no `seg`;
  - non-strict evaluation: discarded sub-terms are never evaluated;
  - a whole-segment interpolation is joined like `/`.

  [`docs/SEMANTICS.md`](../SEMANTICS.md) states all of this as a contract. [`tests/contract_test.py`](../../tests/contract_test.py) checks it with a reference evaluator that shares no code with the implementation.
- **One segment after the head, by default.** A parameter joined below a path binds one
  segment unless declared `SubPath`. Every common expression thereby has a static shape, and
  `root / user_input` cannot traverse. This is Part 1's `Under[ROOT]` safety, obtained from Part
  4's need for structure rather than from a type system.
- **The reverse direction.** `match` and `match_all` recover typed bindings and verify each one
  by rendering it back. They raise `AmbiguousMatchError` rather than guess. `glob` walks a tree
  along an expression's structure, and `Lens` maps paths between two layouts. This is Part 1's
  lens, with `backward` given by parsing.
- **Compilation, in Python.** `compile()` generates one function per expression and falls back
  to the interpreter on any exception, so errors are identical by construction. `wend.frame`
  lowers to Polars expressions where Polars reproduces Python's formatting exactly.

  **[Fact]** In `bench/bench.py`, compiled rendering takes about 1.8–2.1 µs, against 3.5–4.4 µs for eager pathlib, and a validated Polars row about 0.24–0.29 µs. These were measured on one 4-vCPU container, so only comparisons within a run are meaningful. Part 5's goal, cheap repeated resolution, was met without Rust.
- **Inference.** Part 1's genson-for-filesystems became `wend.infer`. It tokenises names into
  digit skeletons, chooses round-trippable format specs, collapses similar directories into
  segment fields, and reports optional entries.

The audit and the messy-tree testing then tested the rebuild against its own claims ([audit-22](2026-10-17-audit-22.md), [messy-trees-22](2026-10-17-messy-trees-22.md)). **[Fact]** Four of their findings bear on the design rather than on details:

- **A traversal bypass.** A parameter that headed its own expression kept path semantics
  through a deferred operation. As a result, `P("/srv/static") / P(name).with_suffix(".png")`
  rendered `name="../../etc/passwd"` outside the base. Joining now re-applies the operations to
  the parameter as a segment, and a containment property over random terms guards the fix.
- **Wrong optional flags.** Inference's optional flags were wrong in both directions on real
  trees. They are now defined on the patterns and computed by walking each pattern down the
  observed tree.
- **Collisions in lossless lenses.** A lens could be "lossless" while sending two sources to
  one target. A lossless lens now checks that each target maps back uniquely.
- **Formatting fidelity.** A t-string formatting probe of 54 cases differed from f-string
  formatting only in the structural cases that SEMANTICS.md §4.1 now lists. One inconsistency
  was fixed: `t"data/{x}"` rejected `x=3` while `t"data/{x}.csv"` formatted it.

## 4. Two sets of five

The essays and the PRs are both five-part, and it is tempting to read them as one series implementing the other. They are not. **[Interpretation]** The essays are ordered *conceptually*, from meaning (types, relations) to mechanism (syntax, theory, execution). The PRs are ordered by *dependency*: core, then discovery, then a backend, then inference, then documentation. The mapping between them is many-to-many and uneven:

| essay | where it landed | what remains |
|---|---|---|
| 1 Semantic Types | Kinds and the single-segment default give traversal safety (#18). Lenses (#19). Schema inference (#21) | File/dir and existence types, `Under[ROOT]` as a declared constraint, capabilities, effects, lineage, content addressing, `PathQ`, declared directory schemas, anchors, fsspec |
| 2 Connectivity | Substitution: `bind`, `rebase`, static `relative_to` (#18) | Base tracking, `sibling`/`cousin` as relations, dependents moving with a rebase, composition rules |
| 3 Deferred Path-ification | Essentially all of it, made sound (#18) | Readymade counter templates |
| 4 Compilation Theory | Its conclusion ("Python is our parser") and its correctness concern, answered by a normal form and a contract instead of a rewrite system (#18) | What optimisation can still buy: shared work across expression sets, sound rewrites of deferred chains, a cost model |
| 5 Lazy Execution | Its goal, in Python: `compile()`, batch `many`/`columns`, Polars (#18, #20) | The Rust arena, optimiser and slot machine, at a boundary measurements justify |

**[Interpretation]** Three things follow from this mapping:
- The rebuild realised the *mechanism* half of the programme, and the discovery side of Part 1, more completely than the essays imagined: reverse matching appears in none of them.
- It left the *meaning* half (Parts 1 and 2) mostly unbuilt.
- Where an essay's method proved unsound or unnecessary, the essay's goal was kept and its method replaced. Rewriting gave way to a normal form, and Rust to generated Python.

## 5. The roadmap the journal already recorded

Before proposing anything, here is what the four entries themselves say is next.

- **The retrospective's [*Open threads*](2026-10-17-retrospective-17.md#open-threads)** listed
  eight proposals, plus a lowering design ([*A lowering that keeps both*](2026-10-17-retrospective-17.md#a-lowering-that-keeps-both))
  and two performance proposals:
  - Delivered by the rebuild:
    - the semantics choice;
    - reverse matching and `glob`;
    - filesystem inference;
    - substitution as the single primitive;
    - parameter kinds;
    - t-string preservation and compile targets in Python and Polars.
  - Not delivered:
    - the Part 1/2 surface (existence and kind checks, `Under[ROOT]`, `sibling`/`cousin`, lineage);
    - the value-typed Rust lowering;
    - a Polars plugin for the operations `pl.format` can't express.
- **The rebuild's [*Open questions for the owner*](2026-10-17-rebuild-22.md#open-questions-for-the-owner)**:
  - review the hybrid semantics and the single-segment default (since provisionally approved);
  - release as 0.2.0;
  - Windows semantics;
  - Part 1 capabilities beyond kinds, existence checks "would fit naturally as glob/resolve options".

  Its *Measurements* section proposed a native backend only for batch matching and filesystem walks that return Arrow. Its *Missing* section adds fast-path coverage, Polars path normalisation and variable-depth inference.
- **The audit's [*Decisions for the owner*](2026-10-17-audit-22.md#decisions-for-the-owner) and
  [*Residual risks*](2026-10-17-audit-22.md#residual-risks)**:
  - make `SEMANTICS.md` the reviewed specification, and require contract-test changes with it;
  - extend property coverage to dynamic specs, `Text`-derived fields and deferred heads;
  - the 30 µs construction cost.
- **The messy-tree entry's [*Missing*](2026-10-17-messy-trees-22.md#missing)**:
  - alternation in patterns;
  - a `glob` mode that reports ambiguity and continues;
  - generalisation across opaque sibling families.
- **Every *Divergence* section since the rebuild** records that `pyproject.toml` says 0.1.1
  while PyPI's 0.1.1 is the old, unsound code.

**[Interpretation]** Read together, the recorded plan has four strands:
1. Settle and publish what exists: ratify the contract, release.
2. Extend toward Part 1 through *effects at explicit boundaries* (existence and kind checks as `glob`/resolve options), rather than through a type system first.
3. Close the expressiveness and robustness gaps that testing on real trees exposed.
4. Pursue performance only where measurement points: construction cost and per-entry matching overhead, and native code only at batch boundaries.

Two things are absent from that plan:
- **Connectivity** (Part 2) appears only as a line item, "`sibling`/`cousin`", never as a priority.
- **Optimisation by rewriting** appears nowhere after the rebuild, because the normal form took its place.

A review after the merge suggested a different order: relationships, then types, then inference, then optimisation, then native execution. That order follows the essays more closely than the journal does. It agrees with the journal on types and inference, puts connectivity first where the journal never ranked it, and includes rewrite-based optimisation, for which the journal recorded no evidence of need. Both orders are recorded here so the choice is explicit rather than drifted into.

## 7. Questions that remain open

These are research questions, not tasks. Each is recorded in an issue so that it travels with the work.

- **Permanence of the semantics.** Is the hybrid contract wend's identity or a 0.2 decision? The single-segment default and native formatting of `None` at segment positions are the most consequential parts (#24).
- **What a relation is.** A new type, an attribute of `PathExpr`, or a small graph of named expressions? Do bases chain or flatten, and where does a path algebra stop and a graph library begin (#26)?
- **Purity and effects.** `render` is pure, and that purity underwrites the contract. Existence checks, fallback chains, scaffolding and anchors are effects. The open design question is the shape of the explicit realisation step they share (#27, #29, #32, #39).
- **Static or runtime types.** Python lacks dependent types. How much of `Under[ROOT]` and of capabilities a type checker can see is an empirical question about pyright and ty (#27).
- **Unions and purity.** Part 4 describes paths as pure, branch-free expressions. A union is a branch (#29).
- **What optimisation buys after the normal form** (#36), and **which native boundary pays** (#37). Both are to be answered by measurement.
- **Inference beyond data layouts.** On code and system trees inference describes rather than specifies. Whether variable-depth and cardinality inference change that is open (#31).

## 8. Proposed order

**[Proposal]** The first three steps:

1. **#24, ratify the contract.** It is small. Every later change either amends `SEMANTICS.md` or must be tested against it. Its property gaps (dynamic specs, `Text`-derived fields, deferred heads) are where the next unsoundness is most likely to hide, as the deferred-operation traversal bug showed.
2. **#25, release.** The package users can install today is the unsound 0.1.1. Shipping the rebuild removes a live hazard and closes the oldest recorded divergence. It is the owner's decision, and it need not wait for every open question: a 0.x version leaves room.
3. **#26, connectivity.** It is the largest part of the original vision with no implementation. Containment (#27), schemas (#32) and lineage (#28) all build on a notion of *base*. The journal never ranked it; this proposal follows the essays and the post-merge review here, and says so.

After that, the recorded plan suggests a sequence:
- #27, as explicit check steps first;
- #30 and #29, from real-tree evidence;
- #31 and #32 together, since inference should emit what schemas declare;
- #35 before #36 and #37, so that performance work follows measurement.
