# 2026-10-22: Drafts: who can reach an unpublished output (research)

The synthesis (§5) found that another process can name an unpublished draft through
`/proc/<pid>/fd`, and narrowed "unnameable until published" to other uids, or a
non-dumpable run. The owner asked for the guarantee to be corrected properly:

> threat model; which processes can access or publish a draft; whether Store relies on
> stronger exclusivity than the kernel provides

and, for the journals generally, to distinguish what needs privilege from what ordinary
users have.

**The answers, in brief.**

1. **A `Draft` is nameless, not private** (§2). Three groups of processes can reach it
   through `/proc/<pid>/fd/N`:
   - the writer's own uid while the writer is dumpable, which is the default;
   - root, always;
   - none other: other unprivileged uids, and the writer's own uid once it is
     non-dumpable.

   Each process that can reach it can do all four things: read it, write into it, give
   it a name (partial contents and all, outliving the writer), and take the writer's
   descriptor. Nothing in between was found. The synthesis measured only "names it".
2. **`Store` relied on more exclusivity than the kernel gives** (§3). The kernel enforces
   one thing: each version name holds one directory (`RENAME_NOREPLACE`). The protocol
   assumed more:
   - the hidden directory a writer fills is the one it claims;
   - nobody writes into it;
   - nobody claims it first.

   None of that held against processes able to rename in the store's directory. Those
   are the writer's uid, root, and any other uid with write permission on a directory
   that is not sticky. The hidden directory was also readable by every user (mode 0755).
   Now:
   - it is private (0700) while it is filled;
   - it is held by its descriptor;
   - before and after the claim, the commit checks by identity that the directory
     claimed is this draft's.

   Each move, replacement or early claim is *detected* and returns no `Version`. None is
   *prevented*: that would need the kernel to condition a rename on identity, which it
   cannot (synthesis §2).
3. **What ordinary users have, and what needs privilege** (§4). The table there is the
   answer to the journals' standing request.

Code: `src/wend/_experimental/publish.py` (`Draft(held=)`, `VersionDraft`, `Store`'s
docstring); `tests/drafts_test.py` (13 tests); the harness gains seven mutants (§5).

Labels as in the other entries. **[Measured]** here means Linux 6.18, ext4, as root
acting as the users 65534 and 65533 unless stated. No Yama LSM was present:
`/proc/sys/kernel/yama` is absent.

## 1. Threat model

**Assets of an unpublished output:**
- **confidentiality**: its partial contents;
- **integrity**: that what is published is what the writer wrote;
- **exclusive publication**: that only the writer gives it a name, and only when it is
  complete.

**Actors**, by what the kernel lets them do:
- **another process of the writer's uid**, unprivileged. The writer may be dumpable (the
  default for a process started as that user) or not (`prctl(PR_SET_DUMPABLE, 0)`, or any
  process that changed its uid: §2);
- **a process of another uid**, unprivileged. It may or may not have write permission on
  the directories involved;
- **root**, or any holder of `CAP_SYS_PTRACE`, `CAP_DAC_OVERRIDE` or `CAP_FOWNER`;
- **the writer's own process**: other threads, forked children holding its descriptors.

  It is trusted: nothing here could separate it from the writer.

**Out of scope:** the kernel, the block device, a hypervisor (as in the leases entry's L4).

## 2. A `Draft`: the matrix

**[Measured]** (`test_a_draft_is_open_to_processes_of_its_own_uid`,
`test_a_non_dumpable_writer_s_draft_is_closed_to_its_own_uid`,
`test_a_draft_is_closed_to_other_users`, `test_root_reaches_every_draft`). The writer is a
`Draft` holding the bytes `partial`. Another process tries four things through
`/proc/<pid>/fd/N`:
- **read**: open it and read;
- **write**: open it for writing and write `P` at offset 0;
- **name**: `linkat(…, AT_SYMLINK_FOLLOW)` into a directory it may write;
- **take**: `pidfd_getfd`.

| other process | writer dumpable (default) | writer non-dumpable |
|---|---|---|
| same uid | reads, writes, names, takes | `EACCES` ×3, take `EPERM` |
| another uid | `EACCES` ×3, take `EPERM` | `EACCES` ×3, take `EPERM` |
| root | reads, writes, names, takes | reads, writes, names, takes |

The named copy reads `Partial`: partial, and changed by the other process before the
writer published anything. It outlives the writer, which abandoned its draft. Every
denial comes from one check: opening `/proc/<pid>/fd/N`, and `pidfd_getfd`, require
`ptrace` access to the writer. Read access suffices for the first; `pidfd_getfd` needs
attach access.

**Consequences:**
- **Publication is not exclusive** against the actors in the "reaches" cells. One of
  them can name the draft under the name the writer means to publish. The writer's
  `publish(policy="create")` then fails with `EEXIST`, and the name holds the writer's
  own partial object. (**[Interpretation]**, from "names" in the matrix; not run as its
  own test.)
- **A process that changed its uid is non-dumpable by default.** **[Measured]** With
  `fs.suid_dumpable` 0, as here, a process that is dumpable (`PR_GET_DUMPABLE` 1) reads
  0 after `setresuid`. A daemon that drops root to its own uid has same-uid draft
  privacy without asking. A process started as that user does not.
- **The setting is process-wide.** Dumpability also governs core dumps, the ownership of
  `/proc/<pid>/*`, and every other process's `ptrace` access. A library must not change
  it. `Draft`'s docstring now states the guarantee and leaves the choice to the host.

**A measurement artifact, recorded so it is not mistaken for a result.** The first probe
reported `EXDEV` for "name" from every actor. Python's `os.link` without `dst_dir_fd`
calls `link(2)`, and Linux's `link(2)` does not follow the final symlink: it tried to
hard-link the `/proc` magic link itself, across filesystems. `linkat(2)` with
`AT_SYMLINK_FOLLOW`, as `Draft.publish` uses, links the file. The synthesis's earlier
measurement had used `dst_dir_fd`, and its result stands.

## 3. A `Store`'s hidden draft: what it relied on

Before this entry, `VersionDraft`:
1. made `.v{n}.draft-{random}` in the store's directory, mode 0755 (umask applied);
2. made each member there by its *name*, a fresh `Draft(io, dir)` each time;
3. wrote `MANIFEST`, then renamed the hidden *name* to `v{n}` with `RENAME_NOREPLACE`;
4. on any failure, deleted whatever the hidden name led to.

**[Measured]** Against processes that may rename in the store's directory:

| what they do | before | now | test |
|---|---|---|---|
| list the store's directory | the hidden name is listed | the same: names are not private | `test_other_users_see_a_hidden_version_s_name_but_not_its_contents` |
| read a member of a hidden draft (another uid) | read | `EACCES` (0700 while filled) | the same; `test_a_hidden_version_is_private_while_it_is_filled` |
| claim the hidden draft first, as `v{n}` | the commit's rename fails, `ENOENT`; the cleanup then fails too, on a name that is gone | detected: `Inconsistent`, the version left as it is, no `Version` returned | `test_a_draft_claimed_by_another_process_is_detected_and_left`, `test_a_draft_claimed_as_its_claim_is_made_is_left` |
| move the hidden draft away and put their own directory at its name | later members go into theirs, and theirs is claimed as `v{n}` | members go into the draft's own directory (held); the claim is refused; theirs is untouched | `test_a_replaced_hidden_directory_is_neither_filled_nor_claimed` |
| do the same between the commit's check and its rename | theirs is claimed | detected after the claim (`Inconsistent`, "not this version"); not prevented | `test_a_directory_put_in_place_as_the_claim_is_made_is_detected` |
| claim it as another uid, the store's directory world-writable | succeeds, even with the draft 0700: renaming within a parent needs write permission on the parent only | the same, detected at commit | `test_another_user_who_may_write_the_store_can_claim_a_draft[False]` |
| the same, the store's directory sticky (`01777`) | refused, `EPERM` | refused; the writer's claim succeeds | `[True]` |

While the early claim lasts, a reader that pins `v{n}` finds no `MANIFEST` and fails
(`FileNotFoundError`). It does not read a partial version as a whole one. A reader
holding the manifest digest from the writer's commit never gets one, because the commit
returned no `Version`.

**[Refuted]** The `Store` docstring's "nothing of this version was ever visible". The
hidden draft was visible, by name to anyone who could list the store's directory, and by
content to anyone who could read it. It now says: nothing visible *under a version's
name*.

**[Proven]** What the kernel gives `Store`, and what it does not:
- **Given.** At most one directory at each `v{n}`, against every process (`RENAME_NOREPLACE`
  is atomic). A writer's claim either installs a directory or fails. Readers who go by
  version names see a version only once claimed.
- **Not given.** That the directory claimed is the writer's, complete and unwritten.
  `rename` is by name, so there is no "rename this directory object if it is still at this
  name". Between any check of identity and the rename, the name can change. The
  post-claim check detects the change; nothing can prevent it.
- **Hence**, against processes that may rename in the store's directory:
  - claim exclusivity rests on their following the protocol;
  - integrity rests on readers verifying against the writer's manifest digest (a
    same-uid process can rewrite `MANIFEST` itself);
  - confidentiality from other uids rests on mode 0700 (the writer's own uid is not
    kept out by mode).

  Who may rename there:
  - the writer's uid and root, always;
  - other uids with write permission on the store's directory, unless it is sticky and
    they own neither the entry nor the directory.

**[Proposal]** A store meant to be shared between users should be a sticky directory
(like `/tmp`), or writable only by its writers' group, whose members are trusted to
follow the protocol. `Store` does not check this. It could warn when its directory is
writable by others and not sticky.

## 4. What ordinary users have, and what needs privilege

Each row says which mechanism gives the guarantee, and who can have it.

| guarantee | an ordinary user has | needs privilege | evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| a draft is unreadable, unwritable and unnameable by other uids | yes: `O_TMPFILE` and `ptrace`'s check, by default | no | §2 |
| … and by processes of the writer's own uid | only by making the writing process non-dumpable (process-wide), or by writing as a dedicated uid | no | §2 |
| … and by root | no | no one can have it | §2 |
| a hidden version's contents unreadable by other uids | yes: mode 0700 | no | §3 |
| one directory per version name | yes: `RENAME_NOREPLACE` | no | synthesis §2.2 |
| the claimed directory is the writer's | detection only (identity checks) | no one can prevent it | §3 |
| other uids cannot claim or replace a hidden version | yes, if the store's directory is not writable by them, or is sticky | no | §3 |
| a committed member cannot change | no: the owner can always `chmod` and write | `CAP_LINUX_IMMUTABLE` (`FS_IMMUTABLE_FL`); an ordinary user's `commit(immutable=True)` is refused with `EPERM` and leaves nothing (`test_an_ordinary_user_cannot_make_a_version_immutable`) | synthesis §3 |
| a change to a committed member is detected | yes: the manifest digest from the commit | no | synthesis §3 |
| a leased input is one version throughout a step | yes, for files the user owns (on `LEASABLE` filesystems) | `CAP_LEASE` for others' files | leases §4–§5 |
| a version is not reclaimed under a reader | cooperative: `flock`, among processes that pin | no | synthesis §3 |
| a sealed copy cannot change | yes: memfd seals | no | #113, synthesis §4.3 |

## 5. Tests, mutants, and a harness defect

**[Measured]** `tests/drafts_test.py`:
- 13 tests; 6 of them need root, to act as other users, and skip without it;
- every test asserts what each actor did, not only what was refused. The control in
  `test_a_draft_is_closed_to_other_users` shows the same draft open to its own uid;
- the writers and the other processes are forked from a fresh interpreter, which asserts
  it is single-threaded first. Forking pytest itself warned "multi-threaded": other test
  files import polars, which starts native threads on import (3 at import, 14 after one
  use). A fork there can deadlock the child.

**[Measured]** Mutants added to `experiments/mutation/mutants.py`, each killed by the
named test:

| mutant | killed by |
|---|---|
| the hidden draft made 0755 | the 0700 test, the other-users test |
| the claimed version left 0700 | the same two |
| members added by the hidden name | the replacement test |
| `Draft(held=)` ignored | the replacement test |
| no identity check before the claim | the replacement test |
| no identity check after the claim | the between-check-and-claim test |
| a version another process claimed is removed by the cleanup | both early-claim tests |

**[Fact] A harness defect, found and fixed.** After one harness run the full suite failed
two early-claim tests that passed alone. The cause was stale bytecode:
- the last mutant (`outcome == "abandon"` to `outcome != "claimed"`) has exactly the
  source's size;
- the harness restored the source within the same second;
- the cached `.pyc` records the source's modification time in whole seconds, and its
  size, and both matched;
- so every later import, outside the harness too, ran the mutant.

The harness now restores the source's modification time and runs mutants without writing
bytecode. All 17 mutants were then re-run and met their expectation, and the suite passed
straight after the harness. The leases entry's mutant table was produced before the fix
and reproduced after it.

**[Interpretation]** The audit of #106–#114 found two defects of the earlier harnesses:
- a mutant that does not compile read as a survivor;
- kills counted by any test.

This is a third of the same kind: a harness that reports on code it did not run. Each
check the harness makes now exists because one of these happened.

## 7. Open questions

- **A warning for shared stores** (§3 proposal): whether `Store` should check its
  directory's mode and owner.
- **Writing as a helper.** A draft written by a non-dumpable helper process, the host
  sending it the bytes, would be private from the host's uid without changing the host.
  Its cost is the leases helper's, plus a copy of every byte.
- **Yama.** With `ptrace_scope` 1, `pidfd_getfd` from a non-ancestor is refused, but
  `/proc/<pid>/fd` read access is not: Yama governs attach, not read. Untested here (no
  Yama).
- **Same-uid integrity of `MANIFEST`.** A reader without the commit's digest trusts
  whatever `MANIFEST` is there. Signed manifests would need a key the same uid cannot
  read, which needs another uid.
