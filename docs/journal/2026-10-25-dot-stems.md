# 2026-10-25: Stems of `.` and `..`, written out (#128)

After #127, the next class of `"unknown"` answers on #119's samples that follows one
pattern is a stem of `.` or `..` given to a name whose suffix is unknown:
`({root}/{x}).with_stem("..")`. Such a change has no view (#119), since it may render
the name `..`, which no segment of a view renders. This entry writes the change out by
cases, as #123 and #127 did for parents and names below a `SubPath`. The contract is
`docs/SEMANTICS.md` §15.3 (3).

## 1. What pathlib does

**[Measured]** On Python 3.14 and 3.15, the two versions wend supports and CI runs,
`with_stem(t)` is `with_name(t + suffix)`, where the suffix is the name's last dot
onward, unless every character before that dot is a dot, and then there is none. A
name ending in a dot has the suffix `.`:

| name | `with_stem(".")` | `with_stem("..")` |
|---|---|---|
| `x` | refused (`Invalid name '.'`) | `..` |
| `x.` | `..` | `...` |
| `x.txt` | `..txt` | `...txt` |
| `.x` | refused | `..` |
| `a.b.c` | `..c` | `...c` |

## 2. The cases, and why they are sound

**[Proven]** Let `e` be `with_stem(t)` of a base whose view (#119) ends in a segment,
with `t` either `.` or `..`, and let `H/q` be that view less its last segment.
- **The name has a suffix `s`.** `s` is a dot then text `r`, so `e` renders
  `H/q/{t}.{r}` then what follows. The case `H/q/{t}.{text}`, with `text` bound to
  `r`, renders the same, except where `{t}.{r}` is `..`. A segment never renders `..`,
  so that one string is left to the next case. That happens only for `t = "."` and
  `r = ""`.
- **The name `..`.** The stem `..` gives it to a name without a suffix, and the stem
  `.` to a name whose suffix is `.`. The case writes it as a literal `..` segment:
  `H/q/..`.
- **The stem `.` of a name without a suffix** is refused by pathlib, so `e` renders
  nothing there and needs no case.

The base's view renders what the base renders, and more, so the cases together render
everything `e` renders, under the same binding. They are used only to prove `"never"`
(`_cases_never`), which a case that renders more can never make false. They are
untagged, so they are paired with every case of the other side.

**[Proven]** `text` is named from the operation's fresh key (#124), as a view's text
is. Two such stems of one base, on two sides, therefore share it only where they render
one text under shared bindings.

**[Fact]** Two more places change.
- **Bases ending in literal text with a dot.** `{day}.csv` was already split by its
  suffix (#119), but dot stems were refused there. They are now allowed:
  `with_stem("..")` of `{day}.csv` renders `...csv`, or `..` where `day` renders dots
  alone. The stem `.` of a name without a suffix is dropped as above.
- **Bases ending in a `SubPath`.** #127 writes the change as the change of the
  `SubPath`'s last segment, or of what precedes it. Each of those cases that is again
  a dot stem is now written out in turn, keeping its tag, so correlated pairing (#127)
  still applies.

## 3. Tests and measurements

**[Measured]** `test_the_cases_of_a_dot_stem_render_all_it_renders` checks the cases
themselves. Over 200 generated stems and the pooled bindings, every rendering is one of
the cases' renderings under the same binding: `text` is bound to the suffix less its
dot, and a `SubPath` is split as in #127. There are 15,266 renderings, 9,957 of the stem
`..` and 5,309 of the stem `.`. Of these, 7,671 come from the case of the stem, a dot
and text, and 7,595 from the name `..`.

**[Measured]** `test_dot_stems_hold_for_pooled_bindings` draws a dot stem against
another expression or dot stem. It checks every `"always"` and `"never"` against the
pooled bindings, in both modes of `builtin_values`.

**[Measured]** `test_a_dot_stem_is_written_out` holds the examples, under both
bindings. #119's `test_a_changed_name_may_be_dotdot`, which holds that no answer may
rule out a `..` the operation renders, still passes.
