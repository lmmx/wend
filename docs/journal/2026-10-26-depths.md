# 2026-10-26: Depths through SubPaths and deferred operations (#131)

`relate` has long decided by depth where two remainders hold segments alone
(`docs/SEMANTICS.md` §15.3, 5): each renders a fixed count of segments. Among the
`"unknown"` answers the overlap analysis leaves at MAYBE, or confirms as YES with no
search able to witness it, some are depth arguments that pass through a `SubPath` or a
deferred operation. `({root}/{n:d}.{p}/{s...}).with_suffix("")` renders `2 + |s|`
segments below `{root}`, and `{root}/{n:d}.{p}/{n:d}.{p}/{s...}` renders `3 + |s|`:
never one path. This entry extends the depth rule to whole expressions.

The work was prototyped in two steps, kept on the branches `claude/wip-129-depths` and
`claude/wip-130-depth-bounds`. The first cancelled parts both sides share; the second
bounds what they leave. They land as one rule, since the first is the case of the
second where nothing is left.

Labels: **[Fact]**, **[Measured]**, **[Proven]**.

## 1. A path's depth, written out

**[Proven]** Write the depth of a rendered path as the number of its segments after the
anchor, as `lexically` counts them. For an expression `e`, `_depth` gives a count `n` of
segments, and the *parts of varying count*, each with a sign:
- a literal head contributes none. Any other head (a head parameter, or a deferred
  operation other than those below) is a part of varying count, with sign `+1`;
- a segment contributes one (§3: never `""`, `.` or a separator), and a `SubPath` is a
  part with sign `+1`;
- `with_name`, `with_suffix` and `with_stem` keep their base's count: pathlib replaces
  the last segment, and refuses where there is none;
- a parent has one fewer, where its base has a segment of its own (`n ≥ 1`). pathlib's
  parent drops the last segment, `..` included, wherever there is one;
- `relative_to(x)` has the base's count less `x`'s, its parts with their signs
  reversed. pathlib refuses unless `x` is the base or one of its parents, and then
  drops `x`'s segments; the anchors must match, and the result has none;
- a join onto a path that is always relative has the sum of the two counts. An
  absolute right side replaces the base, so a join whose right side may be absolute
  has no known depth.

Rendering joins relative pieces, which adds their segments. So on every binding, `e`
renders `n` segments plus each part's own count times its sign.

## 2. Bounding two depths

**[Proven]** The depth of `a` less that of `b` is `d = n − k`, plus each part's count
times its sign (`a`'s as they are, `b`'s reversed). Two cases:
- **A part alike on both sides.** Under shared bindings, a part alike on both sides by
  (1) renders one text on both, and so one count; its signs add, and may cancel.
  "Alike" means an equal key and no name defaulted differently on each side.
- **Every other part.** Under independent bindings, and wherever a name has a different
  default on each side, each side's parts are counts of their own.

Every part renders no segments or more. So where every coefficient left is `≥ 0`, the
difference is at least `d`; where every one is `≤ 0`, at most `d`. Hence, wherever both
render:
- they are never the same where the difference cannot be 0. That is where `d > 0` and
  no coefficient is negative, or `d < 0` and none is positive;
- `a` is never strictly inside `b` where its depth cannot exceed `b`'s, that is where
  `d ≤ 0` and no coefficient is positive;
- `a` never contains `b` in the mirror case.

The rule runs on the expressions themselves, before views or cases, and asks nothing
of how the paths align. So it decides pairs the overlap analysis leaves at MAYBE.

## 3. Tests and measurements

**[Measured]** `test_a_depth_counts_the_segments_rendered` checks `_depth` itself
over 300 generated expressions. Each is a base, perhaps ending in a `SubPath`, under up
to two deferred operations (parents, paths made relative to a parameter, changed
names, joins of deferred paths), with parts joined on. On every pooled binding, the
rendered path has `n` segments plus each part's count times its sign. Of the 300:
- 286 have a known depth;
- 63 involve a path made relative, and 9 a join;
- in all, 26,691 renderings.

**[Measured]** Deterministic tests:
- `test_depths_decide_with_shared_subpaths`:
  - the example above, decided under shared bindings and not under independent ones;
  - a parent beside a changed name;
  - `{s}/{s}` against `{s}/a`, which are one path where `s` is `a`, so multiplicity
    counts;
  - a `SubPath` defaulted differently on each side, which decides nothing.
- `test_depth_bounds_decide_through_signs`:
  - `d/{s...}/a/b` against `d/x`, under either binding;
  - a path made relative to `{p}`, which has two segments at most;
  - a join onto a relative path, which is never `.`;
  - a join onto a path that may be absolute, which may be `/`.

**[Measured]** Mutants of the rule, against `tests/relate_test.py` and
`tests/lang_oracle_test.py` under seed 0:
